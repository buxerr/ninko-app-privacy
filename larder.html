Larder: Privacy Policy

App: Larder: Pantry Inventory & Food Expiry (package com.buddy.larder), Android.
Effective date: 20 October 2026.

In short: Larder has no account, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Your inventory stays on your phone. The only thing the app ever sends is the barcode number of a product you choose to look up, sent to Open Food Facts to find its name.

What stays on your phone

Everything you put into Larder is stored only on your device, in the app’s private storage. That includes item names, brands, barcodes, quantities, dates, notes, locations, households, your shopping list and the products Larder remembers for you, along with your settings. The developer has no server and never receives this data.

If you have Android’s own device backup turned on, Android may include Larder’s data in your personal Google backup or in a transfer to a new phone. That backup belongs to you, and the developer cannot access it.

Backup files (JSON) and spreadsheet exports (CSV) are created only when you ask, and saved wherever you choose with the system file picker. Larder does not upload them anywhere.

Product lookups (Open Food Facts)

When you scan or type a barcode and your phone is online, Larder asks the Open Food Facts database (world.openfoodfacts.org) for the product’s name, brand and pack size. The request contains:

  • the barcode number;
  • your phone’s language code, to get the name in your language;
  • the app’s name and version with the developer’s contact email (Open Food Facts asks apps to identify themselves this way).

It contains no name, account, advertising ID or device identifier. Like any web request, it reaches Open Food Facts from your internet connection’s IP address. Open Food Facts handles that request under its own privacy policy. The answer is kept on your phone, so the same barcode isn’t looked up again for a while.

Lookups are optional: you can always type items in by hand, and nothing is sent while you are offline. In-store codes, such as deli, bakery or weighed goods labels, are never looked up. The connection is encrypted (HTTPS).

Camera

The camera is used only while the barcode scanner is open, and only after you allow it. Barcodes are decoded on your phone by an open-source library (ZXing) built into the app. Camera images are analysed in memory and are never saved, uploaded or shared. You can deny camera access and still type barcodes and items in by hand.

Notifications

Expiry reminders are local notifications. A once-a-day check on your phone, at the time you choose, decides what to remind you about. Nothing is sent anywhere to do this. On Android 13 and newer, Android asks you for permission first. You can turn reminders off in Larder’s Settings or in Android’s settings.

Purchases

Larder Pro is a one-time purchase handled entirely by Google Play. Larder asks Google Play on your device whether you own Pro, and remembers the answer on the phone. The developer does not receive your payment details. Google’s privacy policy applies to purchases.

What Larder does not do

  • No accounts, sign-in or cloud sync.
  • No advertising, advertising ID, analytics, crash-reporting or tracking SDKs.
  • No location, contacts, microphone, photos or file access beyond files you pick yourself.
  • No selling or sharing of personal data.

Deleting your data

Delete items, locations or households in the app, or remove everything by clearing Larder’s storage or uninstalling it. Because the developer holds no copy, there is nothing else to delete.

Children

Larder is a household utility intended for adults (18+). It is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect any information from children.
